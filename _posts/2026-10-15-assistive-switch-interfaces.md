Accessibility switches only help when they can talk to the devices a student actually uses. Assistive Switch Interfaces (https://mrhunsaker.github.io/Assistive_Switch_Interfaces/) provides C++ code for the ESP32 that lets a microcontroller drive assistive switches and touch-controlled devices. It is a practical foundation for building custom switch interfaces around the specific access needs of a student rather than relying on a single off-the-shelf solution.

You can try the live documentation at https://mrhunsaker.github.io/Assistive_Switch_Interfaces/ or browse the source at https://github.com/mrhunsaker/Assistive_Switch_Interfaces.
